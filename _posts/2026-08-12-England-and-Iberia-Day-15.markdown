Sherri and I were off the ship early-ish to explore, get food, and get our steps.

We had a loose plan to follow a circuitous trail along the coast toward [The Tower of Hercules](https://www.coruna.gal/the-tower/en). It's the oldest Roman lighthouse still in service, and a UNESCO World Heritage site, so it seemed worth a look. After about 20 minutes, we started to get hungry for breakfast and diverted into the central part of town -- still on the way towards the lighthouse. 

We peeked into a few cafes before deciding on [El Chef Urbano](https://www.elespanol.com/quincemil/vivir/gastrocoruna/20260514/abre-coruna-chef-urbano-nuevo-local-brunch-brioches-cafe-osos-gigantes/1003744244304_0.html), on Avenida de Navarra up in Monte Alto.

## Total solar eclipse

Funny story, we didn't even know this was an "eclipse cruise" until two days after we booked the cruise. We waited too long to book and so by early June when we sat down to do it, there weren't a lot of options. This one fit the bill perfectly: 14 nights (to make the long travel worth it), Celebrity (a brand we know and like), interesting ports, and our preferred type of stateroom. Only once I started to look at the itinerary more closely did I make the realization. And then I started to get excited. What a bonus!

Onboard, as you can see yesterday, there was an informational lecture on what eclipses are and what to expect for this one. The staff made preparations for how to position the ship, and how to communicate that. The ship provided eclipse glasses for everyone. We were ready!

We had a sit-down meal at one of the restaurants around 6:30, securing a seat by the window on the port side[^2], mostly by luck. As we ate and looked out the window, we formed the idea this might be a good place to watch the eclipse. We didn't know how crowded the top decks would be, and here was a front-row seat... with drink and dessert service! 

We conspired with the waiter that our plan was to eat slow and kill time until the eclipse. He was onboard. There were _lots_ of empty tables at this point.

As time went on, though, we realized we'd miss the partial eclipse, since that was happening to the aft. We ultimately decided to head up to the top deck and take our chances with the crowds.

It ended up being a much better choice than staying at our window spot. I'd expected the top deck to be wall-to-wall crowded, people jostling for position, but it wasn't like that at all. We had plenty of room to view the partial eclipse from the aft before they turned the ship, and then had a nice spot up top next to one of the hot tubs for totality. Having people crowded nearby was, for me, a feature not a bug. The excitement was contagious.

I made a conscious effort to be in the moment once totality hit. It's something I'll remember forever.
