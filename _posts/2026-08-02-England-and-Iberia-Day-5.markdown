Sea day. Since sea days are boring, I'll use this one to talk about when we scheduled this cruise, and what my feelings are on Celebrity Edge-class ships and their infinite verandas. 

## Infinite Verandas

This is a Celebrity Edge-class ship. On these ships, there are not a lot of traditional veranda rooms. Instead, Celebrity offers an **"Edge Stateroom with Infinite Veranda"**. 

What is that? A standard veranda room like you might be used to has an outside sitting area with a few chairs and maybe a table, with a door separating inside from out. For the Edge-class of ships, Celebrity has this hybrid inside/outside kind of stateroom. The entire end of the cabin is window, with the entire top part able to slide down. 

[Here's a video tour of an Infinite Veranda stateroom](https://youtu.be/RQh8kiGl8Xo).

I prefer traditional balconies over these edge veranda rooms, for a few reasons:

- When you open the giant window, the air conditioning shuts off. From our experience in the Caribbean in January, this means the stateroom gets pretty hot pretty fast. You wanted fresh air but now you're making the other person in the room accommodate you.
- Even with the window closed, this part of the room gets pretty warm when in direct sunlight. 
- You can separate the 'veranda' from the rest of the room with a full-length curtain, but that doesn't stop noise, and you still have the AC issue if you also open the window. 
- As far as I can tell, there aren't any infinite veranda staterooms that _also_ have upper berths. As a parent with two teenagers, two separate beds is strongly preferred over a convertible sofa that sleeps two. In fact, it's a non-starter for us. 
- Those chairs aren't all that comfortable. 
- There's a motor-actuated shade at the window instead of a blackout curtain. This means there's no peeking your head out to see where you are. You can start to raise the shade... from the bottom... but it's much less convenient. 

The only itinerary where I'd consider an infinite veranda would be Alaska, where more reasonable temperatures and an abundance of scenery play well with this configuration. Otherwise, it's a pass for me.

Enough about that. For this cruise, we booked it quite late -- June 14, with the sailing on August 1. As such, there weren't many choices for staterooms. By some miracle, I was able to find two oceanviews adjacent to each other on deck 3. Sherri and Siena were already familiar with this cabin configuration from our January cruise, where they liked the layout and the location. We opted for the two cabins so everyone could get a proper bed. We split between me and my son in one cabin, and Sherri and Siena in the adjoining. 

We all slept in, still working through the ravages of jet lag. I slept solid from 10pm to 8:30am or so, and yet my Apple Watch[^1] was convinced I'd had a bad night and was up a few times.

I had a light breakfast at the high-top tables in the back of the buffet with Sherri, and did the same for lunch a bit later. I laid out for a while by myself, and then with Sherri and Siena.

Around 4:30 I went up to [Eden](https://www.celebritycruises.com/things-to-do-onboard/eat-and-drink/restaurants-and-cafes/eden-restaurant) to enjoy the view and update this log. We'll likely do 6pm dinners for the rest of this cruise. There's no waiting to get into a restaurant at that hour and the service is fast.

A few other random tidbits:

A la carte drink prices are expensive. I haven't tracked drink prices over time, but $12 for a Bud Light? Or $16 for a Stella? I'll cover this towards the end of the overall trip report, or perhaps in a separate article, but this was the first cruise where I felt there was an excessive amount of nickel-and-diming.[^2]
