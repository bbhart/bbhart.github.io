We all slept terribly, victims of jet lag. We were out of the hotel by 7:50am to make the walk down to Waterloo for our 8:35am train to Southampton. It's an easy walk, and we had ample time to pop into a coffee shop for croissants to take on the train.

We arrived early enough at Waterloo that the board only told us our train would be somewhere in Platforms 12-29. We walked over to that area and our platform was assigned a few minutes later. Two of our people tapped in with their contactless cards instead of scanning their prepurchased tickets, but we caught it and they were able to tap out a moment later and re-scan without any trouble.

It's an easy ride to Southampton Central, no transfers needed.
<img src="/assets/20260801-bankside-train-calling-points.jpg" width="50%" alt="Phone screenshot at 7:30am of a rail app's Calling Points screen for the 08:35 London Waterloo train to Weymouth. There's zero convenient luggage storage for roller bags on these trains, unlike other systems I'd used in Switzerland, Germany, Japan, Norway... You either have to be prepared to hog seats with bags or to lift suitcases up and over your head to wedge them into the overhead racks. Ours fit, but barely. Note: this was one of the two first class cars. Perhaps there are other cars with better luggage storage? Anyway, we all sat at a four-top table. The ride was fine. I was too tired to enjoy it. It was transportation.

We got off at Southampton Central along with another older couple -- Dottie (a retired nurse) and her husband -- who turned out to be on our same cruise. We'd mentioned our plan to walk to the terminal and they said they were game. Google Maps wasn't being especially helpful, as it was tough to parse whether we should be walking along the car park or going up the hill on the main road. There's a convenient cruise terminals sign, but ONLY after you've already committed to one direction. Once we were heading the right way, the walk was straightforward.

We arrived at the terminal around 10:15. We cleared security, checked in with Celebrity, and took seats in the terminal. Waiting in the terminal was a first-time experience for us, since we're usually arriving after embarkation has already started. They seat you in the order you arrived (Priority and Concierge get their own area) and then board you in that order.

Almost everyone who boarded ahead of us either headed to their rooms or went to check in at their muster stations. We made a beeline for the buffet and, I kid you not, we were the very first people into the buffet on an embark day. It was eerie. Embark day buffet is usually a shit show.

The rest of the day was uneventful -- regular cruise stuff.
