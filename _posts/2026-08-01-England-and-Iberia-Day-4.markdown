We all slept terribly, victims of jet lag. We were out of the hotel by 7:50 to make the walk down to Waterloo for our 8:35am train to Southampton. It's an easy walk, and we had ample time to pop into a coffee shop for croissants to take on the train.

We arrived early enough at Waterloo that the board only told us our train would be somewhere in Platforms 12-29. We walked over to that area and our platform was assigned a few minutes later. Two of our people tapped in with their contactless cards instead of scanning their prepurchased tickets, but we caught it and they were able to tag out a moment later and re-scan without any trouble.

There's zero convenient luggage storage for roller bags on these trains, unlike in Switzerland. You either have to be prepared to hog seats with bags or to lift 40 pounds up and over your head to wedge them into the overhead racks. They fit, but barely. We all sat at a four-top table. The ride was fine. I was too tired to enjoy it. It was transportation.

We got off at Southampton Central along with another older couple -- Dottie, a retired nurse, and her husband -- who turned out to be on our same cruise. We'd mentioned our plan to walk to the terminal and they said they were game. Google Maps wasn't being especially helpful, as it was tough to parse whether we should be walking along the car park or going up the hill on the main road. There's a convenient cruise terminals sign, but ONLY after you've already committed to one direction. Once we were heading the right way, the walk was straightforward.

---

**For future travelers**, here's the walk from Southampton Central Station to the Horizon cruise terminal:

Exit the train station through the south exit. We were already on the southernmost platform and had an easy exit; you may need to go up and over some tracks to reach it. Once outside, continue directly south across the car park toward the main road, then turn right onto the sidewalk and head up the hill. At this point you should see a blue sign directing you to Dock Gate 10 and Cruise Terminals. (This is the sign I wish were about ten minutes closer to the train station.)

You'll pass through some nice shade and then follow the sidewalk to the left, and you might catch sight of your ship off in the distance. That's where you're heading. You can stay on this side of Southern Road the rest of the way. You'll cross one large intersection with proper walk signals. Further up you'll come to a second intersection that has no walk signals -- look at the traffic lights for the crossing traffic and wait until *all* lanes are red, otherwise you risk getting squished by turning traffic you might not be primed to look for if you're from a right-side-driving country.

Once across, continue through Gate 10; the guard will wave you through. If you're going to the Horizon terminal (102), ease off to the left and cross the street at the earliest opportunity, per the advice of the guard at the gatehouse. From there, continue past the car park and make a right turn to reach the terminal.

It's not a hard walk -- certainly easier than [the walk from the Civitavecchia train station to Largo della Pace](https://bbhart.com/travel/rome/silversea/2026/Rome-and-Silversea-Day-5.html) -- but you'll want decent wheels on your luggage.

---

We arrived at the terminal around 10:15. We cleared security, checked in with Celebrity, and took seats in the terminal. Waiting in the terminal was a first-time experience for us, since we're usually arriving after embarkation has already started. They seat you in the order you arrived (Priority and Concierge get their own area) and then board you in that order.

Almost everyone who boarded ahead of us either headed to their rooms or went to check in at their muster stations. We made a beeline for the buffet and -- I kid you not -- we were the very first people into the buffet on an embark day. It was eerie. It is usually a shit show. We even had time to do our muster check-in along the way.

The rest of the day was uneventful -- regular cruise stuff.
