Back to present day, we wanted to get out into the countryside, so I booked the [St. Kitts Good Time Tour](https://www.viator.com/tours/St-Kitts/Island-Tours-and-Transfers/d930-186665P1) with Shane through Viator. As you'll recall from two days ago in the DR, we booked and had a solid experience with Viator there, so I was a little less stressed about logistics going into this one.

For future readers thinking of visiting St. Kitts, I'd highly recommend this tour. Shane is a smart, funny, and engaging tour guide. His connections and our group size let us experience the island in a way you just can't get on a minibus excursion.


<img src="/assets/20260121-basseterre-port-zante-ships.jpg" alt="The Celebrity Apex and Royal Caribbean's Allure of the Seas docked at the Port Zante cruise pier in Basseterre, seen across large boulders in the morning" width="100%" />

<img src="/assets/20260121-basseterre-taxi-tour-map.jpg" alt="Inside a taxi van, the driver in a red shirt holds up a GoodTimes Tours St. Kitts map marking stops including Brimstone Hill, Romney Manor, Black Rocks, Cockleshell and South Friars Bay" width="100%" />

Our first stop was [Romney Manor](https://www.caribellebatikstkitts.com/), a 17th-century estate (the sign out front says 1626) that's now home to Caribelle Batik, a local workshop that's been hand-dyeing fabric and clothing using the Indonesian batik technique since 1975. 

<img src="/assets/20260121-romney-manor-sign.jpg" alt="A sign at Romney Manor, 1626, Home of Caribelle Batik, surrounded by tropical plants" width="100%" />

<img src="/assets/20260121-romney-manor-cottages.jpg" alt="Bright yellow cottages with orange trim and green-and-orange shutters at Romney Manor, beside a yellow gazebo under palms, with a guide in a red shirt pointing and Brian's wife and daughter looking on from behind, near an Exit Only sign" width="100%" />

<video src="/assets/20260121-romney-manor-cat.mp4" width="100%" controls playsinline muted></video>

Next we made our way to [Brimstone Hill Fortress](https://www.brimstonehillfortress.org/), a massive British stone fort built over about a century starting in 1690 on a hill above the island's west coast, and now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Shane treated us to refreshing beverages and we walked around the ramparts and ruins and enjoyed the sweeping views of the Caribbean. 

<img src="/assets/20260121-brimstone-hill-carib-beer.jpg" alt="Brian in a striped polo and khaki shorts sitting on a stone wall at Brimstone Hill Fortress holding a bottle of Carib beer, with his wife and son on the cobbled parade behind him, a cannon on the rampart, and the green coast and the island of Sint Eustatius on the horizon" width="100%" />

<img src="/assets/20260121-brimstone-hill-ruins.jpg" alt="Roofless stone ruins with arched openings on a grassy slope at Brimstone Hill Fortress, high above the blue Caribbean, with a distant bastion on the hillside to the right" width="100%" />

<img src="/assets/20260121-brimstone-hill-citadel.jpg" alt="The Fort George citadel at Brimstone Hill, a dark stone bastion with cannons and red doors perched on a green hilltop above the open sea under white clouds" width="100%" />

A passing storm drove us into the gift shop for a bit (_very_ convenient 🤔)...
<video src="/assets/20260121-brimstone-hill-rain.mp4" width="100%" controls playsinline muted></video>

... and then, as fast as it came, it was gone again. Next was lunch. Shane offered a few choices for lunch, then called in a to-go order to a local restaurant. We went, picked that up, and made our way to his friend's bar on the beach -- closed during the day, so great for our lunch purposes. Another lighter shower passed through.

Sated, we then made our way to [Timothy Hill](https://www.afar.com/places/timothy-hill-overlook-saint-george-basseterre-parish) overlook, where the Atlantic Ocean and Caribbean Sea meet.

<img src="/assets/20260121-timothy-hill-selfie.jpg" alt="Selfie of Brian in sunglasses and a striped polo at the Timothy Hill overlook, with the narrow green peninsula of southeast St. Kitts behind him, a road winding between the Atlantic surf on the left and calm Caribbean water on the right, and Nevis in the distance" width="100%" />

<img src="/assets/20260121-timothy-hill-overlook.jpg" alt="Brian's daughter from behind, her blonde ponytail blowing, leaning on boulders at the Timothy Hill overlook and looking out over the peninsula, the Atlantic and Caribbean on either side and Nevis on the horizon" width="100%" />

Back in Basseterre we thanked Shane for the tour, wandered around a bit, took our obligatory photo with the Name Of The Place In Giant Colorful Letters signs that are everywhere these days, then called it a day.
