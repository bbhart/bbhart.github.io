- **End of day:** London, England

We were up and operating by mid-morning. The jet lag is real. 

I'd narrowed our brunch choices to [The Table Southwark](https://thetablecafe.com/southwark/)[^1], or [Black Sheep Coffee](https://blacksheepcoffee.co.uk/blogs/locations/southwark) at about a fifth of the cost. We went with Black Sheep. Four pastries and an Americano came to £14, which I was happy with. The service, atmosphere, and quality were solid enough I left a review.

From there we had a mission to find a Sephora, which took us to the Carnaby Street shopping area by Underground. Transit today was simpler than yesterday's Thameslink confusion. We did observe that the Tube doesn't have air conditioning -- maybe it's not an issue most of the year? It's also the most *tube-like* subway system we've been on, or at least the lines we rode today were: low ceilings, narrower cars, not a lot of room on the platform.

Shopping successful, we tubed over toward St. Paul's. 

In the area we found a quick sushi lunch spot at [Wasabi](https://www.wasabi.uk.com/location/chancery-lane) for two of us.

Then onward to St. Paul's. It's a little tricky there, as it's free if you're coming to worship and something like £60 for a family of four if you're just sightseeing. 

<img src="/assets/20260731-st-pauls-cathedral-dome.jpg" alt="St Paul's Cathedral in London photographed from the gardens to the south, showing the full stone dome with its lantern and gold cross, the columned drum beneath, and the pedimented south transept entrance with statues along the roofline. We passed on it, knowing we'd have multiple cathedral opportunities on this trip, and crossed back over the Thames to Southwark. Our group separated for around half an hour so daughter and I could tour [The Golden Hinde](https://www.goldenhinde.co.uk/), which was neat!

Back in our hotel neighborhood, we thought we might go to this place just down from the hotel. However, the mood was _off_ inside, with the bartender clearly not happy we were there even though there were empty tables, we didn't break any etiquette rules I'm aware of, and we were prepared to spend £50 on food. It was noticed by daughter that *nobody* was eating, so overall I'm thinking we dodged a bullet. 

We showed ourselves out and wandered back toward the river, deciding on an early dinner at [Pizza Express](https://www.pizzaexpress.com/). I only had a caprese salad, since this restaurant choice was mostly about what everyone else wanted. Service was a little uneven; food was fine. 

Our hotel is just steps away from the [Tate Modern](https://www.tate.org.uk/visit/tate-modern) and it's **FREE**, so that was next.

We got a late start at the Tate and weren't able to see as much as I would've liked. We made our way back to the hotel.

<img src="/assets/20260731-bankside-illuminated-sign.jpg" alt="Large freestanding letters spelling BANKSIDE, each studded with round bulbs, set up on a paved plaza outside a modern brick and glass building whose entrance canopy is hung with strings of warm fairy lights. Later, around 9:45, I set out to find something to eat, but most restaurants were closed or about to close their kitchens. Does London close early, or was it just our neighborhood? My dinner ended up being a chicken salad sandwich and a bag of Doritos from a convenience store. End of night.
